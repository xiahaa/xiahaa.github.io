Dr. Xiao Hu is a researcher and engineering leader working at the intersection of the low-altitude economy, UAV autonomy, and intelligent transportation. He currently leads a team of 10+ researchers at the Lower Airspace Economy Research Institute of the International Digital Economy Academy (IDEA), developing the Smart Integrated Lower Airspace System (SILAS) and conducting research on city-scale reconstruction and multi-agent planning. He also serves as an Adjunct Assistant Professor at HKUST (Guangzhou). He received his Ph.D. from the Technical University of Denmark (DTU) with a thesis on vision-based positioning for UAVs (GPA 12/12), and brings more than 12 years of academic and industrial R&D experience across IDEA, HKUST(GZ), Alibaba DAMO Academy, TrackMan, DJI, and DTU. His research spans low-altitude spatial infrastructure, perception and localization, multi-sensor fusion, autonomous decision making, motion and mission planning, and applied AI. Algorithms he developed have been deployed in DJI commercial UAVs, including the Phantom 4, Mavic 2, and Inspire 2.

Research Thoughts on the Low-Altitude Economy

The low-altitude economy demands a spatial-intelligence stack that today's systems do not yet provide. Our team's recent results mark important steps toward closing this gap:

NeurIPS 2026SatNav: A Scalable Benchmark for Long-Horizon UAV Vision-Language Navigation from Satellite Imagery
Focusing on long-horizon UAV vision-language navigation, SatNav builds a scalable benchmark from satellite imagery — a key exploration for the direction, providing the evaluation foundation that long-range VLN has lacked.
ACM MM 2026CityPaint: A Free Lunch from 2D Diffusion for City-Scale 3D Texturing
A city-scale 3D texturing framework that requires no city-scale 3D training data. CityPaint exploits 2D diffusion priors, combining adaptive camera selection, Top-K view baking, and terrain–building decoupled refinement to achieve text-controllable, globally consistent generation of large-scale 3D urban scenes.

Together with our earlier accumulation such as OSM-based localization (OSMLoc, ICRA 2026), these pieces have preliminarily connected an end-to-end technical pipeline — from OSM/satellite data to low-altitude 3D spatial reconstruction, localization, and vision-language navigation. This chain effectively alleviates the industry-wide scarcity of real-scene training data for the low-altitude domain, and lays a solid foundation for low-altitude digital twins and simulation for autonomous UAV navigation. My sincere thanks go to all team members for their efforts and dedication — and I look forward to our next breakthroughs.
